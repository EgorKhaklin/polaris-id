The record, wallet by wallet

How it works

Issue. Hold. Verify.

  1. Issue

    An authority signs the credential with ML-DSA-65. Two independent ML-DSA implementations must agree, or issuance stops.

  2. Hold

    The person holds the credential. In zero-knowledge mode, a check is issuer-unlinkable and stores no credential identifier.

  3. Verify

    Anyone checks it offline, against published keys, with no server and no network. Whether it counts right now is answered online, or by a short-lived signed status assertion.

Read the design on GitHub

The rules

The rules live in the database.

  1. The audit record is append-only.Enforced by a trigger and the grants
  2. A zero-knowledge verification stores no credential identifier.Enforced by a check constraint
  3. One person holds at most one active identity token.Enforced by a unique index
  4. Identity is not money.Held by absence

The first three are refusals written into the schema. The fourth is an absence: no monetary table or column exists. All four are checked on every build.

The hard cases

When it goes wrong.

Duress-aware: a second code, quietly on the record.

A second code, built for a coercer who does not know it exists, verifies normally and silently records duress in the append-only audit record.

Lose every device, and come back.

A person who loses every device comes back through a ceremony that needs all three: a biometric check, a sworn statement and a witness from a second authority.

Algorithm migration: two signatures, never none.

The signing algorithm is data. During a move between algorithms a credential carries two signatures, and the database never leaves it without an active one.

Peace of mind, without surveillance.

Our aim is a safer, more peaceful world, where anyone can prove who they are without being watched.
