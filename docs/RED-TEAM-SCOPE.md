# Red-team scope: the Polaris external adversarial engagement

**Reader:** the security firm the operator commissions to attack a deployed
Polaris stack, and the operator who signs the statement of work.
**Job:** define the engagement type, the adversaries to embody, the surfaces
in and out of scope as they exist in the current tree, the deliverables the
maintainers accept, and the disclosure timeline that applies to every finding.

**Status:** scope document. No engagement has been commissioned yet. The
operator takes this document to a firm, negotiates terms, and runs the
engagement; the findings return to the repository under the commitments in
the last section.
**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-02

---

## Engagement type

Time-boxed external red team, two to three calendar weeks, black-box moving
to grey-box:

- **Week 1, black box.** The firm receives a deployment URL and nothing else.
- **Week 2, grey box.** The firm receives this document, the
  [threat model](design/threat-model.md), the
  [architecture overview](ARCHITECTURE-OVERVIEW.md), the
  [PQC posture](reference/PQC-POSTURE.md), and read access to the source. The
  grey-box phase starts with a confirmed read of [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md)
  and the threat model; the firm attacks the documented controls, not the
  surface in general.
- **Week 3, depth.** The firm concentrates on the highest-severity leads from
  the first two weeks and produces executable reproductions.

A continuous purple-team arrangement is an option for a deployed authority,
after the time-boxed engagement has established the baseline.

The target is a stack the operator controls, built from the production
compose files ([docker-compose.prod.yml](../polaris_web/docker-compose.prod.yml)
behind the Caddy edge) or the [Helm profile](../deploy/helm/polaris/values.yaml),
with real secrets generated by
[polaris-generate-secrets.sh](../scripts/polaris-generate-secrets.sh) and no
seed accounts left enabled. A finding against the demo stack that does not
reproduce against the production configuration is reported as informational.

## Threat actors

The firm embodies at least these three profiles. Each has a success criterion
the report scores against.

### TA-1: external criminal

**Capability:** a standard attacker toolkit; no prior knowledge of Polaris;
financially motivated.
**Goal:** authentication bypass; exfiltration of `IdentityToken` or
`Individual` rows; insertion of tokens for resale.
**Success:** any of the above.

### TA-2: coerced insider

**Capability:** a live operator or admin credential, plus an external party
directing its use. This is the actor Polaris exists to resist.
**Goal:** issue or revoke tokens at the coercer's direction; read an
individual's verification history on the coercer's behalf; suppress or
alter the record of having done so.
**Success:** any action against an identity record that leaves no row in
`TokenLifecycleEvent`, `VerificationEvent`, `AuditAccessLog`, or
`AuthAuditLog`. The append-only triggers in
[06_triggers.sql](../polaris_sql/06_triggers.sql) and
[the audit-access migration](../polaris_sql/migrations/2026-05-15-003-audit-access-log.up.sql) and the
[audit-of-record discipline](design/audit-of-record.md) are the controls
under test.

### TA-3: state-level adversary

**Capability:** sophisticated tradecraft; supply-chain reach; possible access
to the custody backend; persistence across patches.
**Goal:** mass issuance of forged tokens; long-term undetected presence in
the deployed stack.
**Success:** undetected presence for more than 7 days, or one token whose
`TokenSignature` verifies under the issuer's public key without the custody
backend having signed it.

## In scope

The surfaces below are regenerated against the current tree. Each names the
file that implements the control and the behaviour the firm attempts to
break.

### Edge

- **The Caddy edge** ([Caddyfile](../polaris_web/Caddyfile), self-built in
  [Dockerfile.caddy](../polaris_web/Dockerfile.caddy)). TLS termination,
  HSTS, the security-header set, the 200 requests per minute per IP rate
  limit, HTTP-to-HTTPS redirect, and the blue-green upstream list. Attempt
  header injection through `X-Forwarded-For`, rate-limit evasion by key
  manipulation, and reaching the app port without passing the edge.
- **Post-quantum key exchange.** The edge negotiates the hybrid
  X25519MLKEM768 group with clients that offer it and falls back to
  classical X25519 otherwise; the group is opportunistic, not required
  ([PQC posture](reference/PQC-POSTURE.md)). Attempt an active downgrade of
  the offered groups and confirm whether the operator can observe it.
- **Certificate issuance.** The
  [CT monitor](../scripts/polaris-ct-monitor.sh) alerts on any certificate
  for the domain outside its allowlist. Attempt to obtain a certificate the
  monitor does not surface.

### Web application

- **Every route in the application**, which since 2026-09-18 is
  [app.py](../polaris_web/app.py) plus the route modules split out of it:
  [rp_api.py](../polaris_web/rp_api.py) (the 36 `/api/v1` routes),
  [atlas_routes.py](../polaris_web/atlas_routes.py),
  [use_case_routes.py](../polaris_web/use_case_routes.py),
  [verification_routes.py](../polaris_web/verification_routes.py) and
  [sql_console.py](../polaris_web/sql_console.py), with role enforcement
  (`admin`, `operator`, `auditor`) as implemented by `require_role` in
  [security.py](../polaris_web/security.py). The
  [authz audit script](../scripts/polaris-authz-audit.sh) lists the expected
  guard per route; a route reachable below its documented role is a
  finding.
- **Authentication.** Password login, the WebAuthn second factor
  ([webauthn_auth.py](../polaris_web/webauthn_auth.py)), the atomic
  failed-login counter (C4), and the recovery-code emergency path
  ([polaris-generate-recovery-code.sh](../scripts/polaris-generate-recovery-code.sh),
  [polaris-recover-admin.sh](../scripts/polaris-recover-admin.sh)).
- **The WebAuthn attestation policy.** `POLARIS_WEBAUTHN_ATTESTATION`,
  `POLARIS_WEBAUTHN_USER_VERIFICATION`,
  `POLARIS_WEBAUTHN_REQUIRE_ATTESTATION`,
  `POLARIS_WEBAUTHN_ALLOWED_AAGUIDS`, and `POLARIS_WEBAUTHN_HARDWARE_ONLY`,
  validated at boot and enforced at registration and assertion
  ([WEBAUTHN-ROLLOUT.md](operator/WEBAUTHN-ROLLOUT.md) Phase 6). Attempt to
  enrol an authenticator the policy excludes, to satisfy an assertion
  without user verification when it is required, and to replay a counter.
- **The operator session registry.** One `OperatorSession` row per login;
  per-role concurrent caps (admin default 3), per-role idle timeouts (admin
  default 30 minutes), the cookie's 8-hour lifetime refreshed on every request, revocation on
  deactivation, and re-validation on every authenticated request
  (`validate_session` in [security.py](../polaris_web/security.py)). Attempt
  to keep a session alive past revocation, to exceed the cap, and to reuse
  a cookie whose registry row is gone.
- **Per-role network policy.** `POLARIS_NETWORK_POLICY_<ROLE>` allow-lists
  evaluated at login and on every live request, with `X-Forwarded-For`
  honoured only behind `POLARIS_TRUST_PROXY`
  ([HARDENING.md](operator/HARDENING.md) section 13). Attempt to spoof an
  allowed address and to use the policy as a password oracle.
- **CSRF and the security headers.** `csrf_protect`, `reject_cross_site`,
  and `apply_security_headers` in
  [security.py](../polaris_web/security.py); the CSP forbids inline scripts
  (C5).
- **Atlas APIs** (`/api/atlas/*`): bounded result sets (C8). Attempt an
  unbounded read.
- **Investigative routes** (`/investigate/token/<id>`,
  `/investigate/individual/<id>`) and every read of an audit-tracked table:
  the read must leave an `AuditAccessLog` row. Reading an identity record
  without one is a TA-2 success.
- **The ZK and anchoring APIs** (`/api/zk/verify`, `/api/zk/epoch/*`,
  `/api/anchor/*`): forged proofs, tampered inclusion proofs, and nonce
  replay against `ZkVerificationNonce`.
- **The federation attestation API** (`/api/federation/attest`,
  `/api/federation/revoke`): verification across agencies without an active
  `AgencyTrustAttestation` row, and transitive trust through a chain of
  attestations, which the verification query refuses (it reads exactly one
  verifier-to-issuer row for the context and never recurses; the schema
  documents this but does not constrain it).
- **The duress path** (`/duress`, `/api/duress/record`): the constant-time
  duress-code comparison and whether a duress event is distinguishable from
  a normal verification on the wire.
- **The read-only SQL console** (`/sql`, admin and auditor): escape from
  read-only, and reads that evade `AuditAccessLog`.

### Database layer

- The schema in [01_schema.sql](../polaris_sql/01_schema.sql), the
  procedures in [05_procedures.sql](../polaris_sql/05_procedures.sql), and
  the triggers in [06_triggers.sql](../polaris_sql/06_triggers.sql).
- **Append-only tables.** Attempt `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on
  `TokenLifecycleEvent`, `VerificationEvent`, `DuressEvent`,
  `AuditAccessLog`, and `AuthAuditLog` through any path, including the
  `polaris.purge_in_progress` carve-out that `uc_archive_purge()` sets.
  `TokenSignature` is immutable except for `deprecation_date`; attempt any
  other mutation.
- **Agency quotas.** `AgencyQuota` caps issuances and revocations per
  rolling day and verifications per rolling hour, enforced by the
  `enforce_agency_quota` trigger on `IdentityToken` inserts, status
  updates, and `VerificationEvent` inserts. The trigger reads no GUC and
  serializes per (kind, agency) with a transaction-scoped advisory lock;
  caps are set with `polaris-id quota-set` in
  [polaris.py](../polaris_cli/polaris.py). Attempt to exceed a cap through
  the procedures, the SQL console, or concurrent writers.
- **The migration framework**
  ([polaris-migrate.sh](../scripts/polaris-migrate.sh),
  [polaris_sql/migrations](../polaris_sql/migrations/README.md)). Attempt to
  roll the schema back below the WebAuthn, session-registry, or quota
  migrations and operate the app against it.
- The `polaris_app` role grants in [09_grants.sql](../polaris_sql/09_grants.sql):
  privilege beyond `SELECT`, `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE` on the application
  tables.

### Key custody

- **[custody.py](../polaris_web/custody.py)**: the `file`, `pkcs11`, and
  `awskms` drivers behind `POLARIS_CUSTODY_DRIVER`
  ([KEY-CEREMONY.md](operator/KEY-CEREMONY.md)). The PKCS#11 key is
  generated non-extractable inside the token and signs with `CKM_ML_DSA`;
  the KMS key is `ML_DSA_65` signing with `ML_DSA_SHAKE_256` and never
  leaves KMS. Attempt to extract the private key, to obtain a signature over
  attacker-chosen bytes through the app, and to substitute a public key so
  a foreign signature verifies. The PIN file (`POLARIS_CUSTODY_PKCS11_PIN_FILE`)
  and the refusal to read the PIN from the environment are in scope.
- **The sealed secret store** ([secretstore.py](../polaris_web/secretstore.py),
  [SECRETS.md, section 5](operator/SECRETS.md#5-the-sealed-secret-store)): the `file`, `age` and `awskms`
  backends, the manifest verification, and the unseal-to-tmpfs path. The
  engagement stack runs with `POLARIS_SECRETS_BACKEND=age` or `awskms`; the
  default `file` backend is plaintext on disk by design and a stack left on
  it is a setup error, not a finding.
  Attempt to read a plaintext secret from disk on a running host.
- **ML-DSA-65 token signatures** through
  [pqc_signing.py](../polaris_web/pqc_signing.py) and the two independent
  verifiers ([two-witness principle](design/two-witness-principle.md)).

### Backup, restore, and disaster recovery

- **pgBackRest** continuous WAL archiving
  ([pgbackrest.conf](../polaris_web/pgbackrest.conf),
  [pgbackrest-conf.sh](../polaris_web/pgbackrest-conf.sh)): the local and
  S3 repositories, the credential fragment that is never read from the
  environment, and repository retention. Attempt to read, alter, or delete a
  backup with the app's credentials, and to poison a WAL segment that the
  restore path replays.
- **The DR path** ([DR.md](operator/DR.md),
  [polaris-dr-drill.sh](../scripts/polaris-dr-drill.sh),
  [DR-DRILLS.md](operator/DR-DRILLS.md)): restore from repository, WAL
  replay, promotion, and the application coming up healthy. Targets are
  RPO at most 300 s and RTO at most 14400 s. Attempt to make a restore
  succeed with a tampered schema or a missing migration, and to make the
  drill report PASS on a corrupted restore.
- **The encrypted `pg_dump` path**
  ([polaris-backup.sh](../scripts/polaris-backup.sh),
  [polaris-restore.sh](../scripts/polaris-restore.sh)) and the streaming
  replication readiness in [FAILOVER.md](operator/FAILOVER.md): the
  `polaris_replicator` role and its `pg_hba` entry.

### Deployment profiles

- **The Docker Compose production stack**
  ([docker-compose.prod.yml](../polaris_web/docker-compose.prod.yml), the
  [blue-green overlay](../polaris_web/docker-compose.bluegreen.yml), the
  [PKCS#11](../polaris_web/docker-compose.custody-pkcs11.yml) and
  [KMS](../polaris_web/docker-compose.custody-awskms.yml) custody overlays),
  the Linux systemd units in [deploy/linux](../deploy/linux/install.sh), and
  the operator scripts in [scripts/](../scripts/polaris-deploy.sh).
- **The Helm profile** ([deploy/helm/polaris](../deploy/helm/polaris/values.yaml),
  [KUBERNETES.md](operator/KUBERNETES.md)): every pod under the restricted
  Pod Security Standard, default-deny NetworkPolicies both directions with
  only the documented edges allowed (DNS egress for every pod, ACME egress
  for caddy, S3 egress for postgres), secrets from an existing `Secret` or
  chart-created. Attempt lateral movement from the app pod to postgres,
  non-DNS egress from any pod other than caddy and postgres, and privilege
  escalation inside a pod.
- **The health and metrics routes** (`/api/health`, `/api/health/ready`,
  `/api/health/live`, `/api/metrics`, `/metrics`): internal-state
  disclosure beyond what the operator intends, including the custody
  status field.
- **The supply chain**: the SBOM and SLSA provenance workflow in
  [sbom.yml](../.github/workflows/sbom.yml) and the base images. The caddy
  ([Dockerfile.caddy](../polaris_web/Dockerfile.caddy)), postgres
  ([Dockerfile.postgres](../polaris_web/Dockerfile.postgres)), and redis
  images are pinned by digest; the app
  ([Dockerfile.prod](../polaris_web/Dockerfile.prod)) and pgbouncer
  ([Dockerfile.pgbouncer](../polaris_web/Dockerfile.pgbouncer)) bases are
  pinned by tag only. Attempt to introduce a dependency the CI gate does not
  catch, and to substitute a tag-pinned base.

### Cryptographic layer

- ML-DSA-65 signing: unforgeable per FIPS 204.
- The Plonky2 Merkle-inclusion circuit in [polaris_zk](../polaris_zk/src/lib.rs)
  and the second witness in [witness2/verifier.py](../polaris_zk/witness2/verifier.py): the
  verifier refuses a forged proof ([zk-soundness](design/zk-soundness.md)).
- Merkle anchoring ([anchoring.py](../polaris_web/anchoring.py)): the
  verifier refuses a tampered inclusion proof.
- Duress codes: the comparison is constant-time.

## Out of scope

- **Physical security** of the deployment host: rack access, physical
  tamper of an HSM. This belongs to the operator's facility, not to Polaris.
- **Social engineering** of operators or maintainers.
- **Volumetric denial of service.** Overwhelming the stack with traffic is
  out of scope. The system is designed for nation-scale steady state, not
  for a 100x burst; the edge rate limit and any upstream scrubbing are
  deployment concerns. Application-level denial of service that a single
  client can cause with a small number of requests (an unbounded query, a
  lock held across a request, write amplification through a trigger) stays
  in scope.
- **Upstream vulnerabilities in dependencies** that already have a patch.
  Report them through the [security policy](../SECURITY.md); do not
  weaponize them against the engagement instance.
- **Seed data and demo accounts.** The `admin / Admin@123!` default is for
  demonstration; its presence on a demo stack is not a finding. Its
  presence on the engagement stack is an operator setup error, reported as
  such.
- **Banking, payments, and merchant codes.** Excluded by C10; a missing
  transaction primitive is by design.

## Rules of engagement

- The target is an instance the operator controls; no other deployment is
  touched.
- The firm does not exfiltrate real personal data. The engagement stack
  carries notional data only.
- Findings are private until the disclosure timeline below runs.
- The firm records every tool and every payload class used so the operator
  can score the stack's detection coverage against them.

---

## Deliverables

The engagement is complete when the firm hands over:

1. **A findings report** with, per finding: severity on the
   [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) scale (Critical, High, Medium, Low), the
   affected component, which of C1 to C10 or which vocation surface is at
   risk, and an executable reproduction, not a narrative.
2. **Per-finding suggested remediation.**
3. **A sanitized engagement timeline** for the post-mortem.
4. **A tool and technique inventory** for the detection-coverage review.
5. **A scored threat-actor summary**: for each of TA-1, TA-2, TA-3, whether
   the success criterion was met and how.

The engagement is successful when the firm produces at least three
documented findings, regardless of severity. Zero findings is treated as
under-scoping or shallow probing, not as a clean bill.

A glossy report without per-finding evidence, a proposal to build a
"security framework" instead of finding vulnerabilities, or a
threat-modeling workshop in place of an attack are not deliverables under
this scope. The threat model already exists; the engagement tests it.

---

## Disclosure timeline

Findings flow to the maintainers first, by the channel in
[SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md). The fix timeline follows the same policy:
Critical within 14 days, High within 30, Medium within 90, Low within 180.
Public disclosure defaults to 90 days from the initial report, extended for
Critical findings as the patch warrants. The firm is credited, with its
consent, in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) at the patch ship.

---

## Maintainer commitments

When an engagement against this scope returns findings, the maintainers
commit to:

1. Record the decision and its rationale for every Critical or High finding,
   including any finding accepted without a fix.
2. Ship each fix under the [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) severity timeline.
3. Document each shipped fix in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) and each
   accepted finding in [known-gotchas.md](../DEVNOTES/known-gotchas.md).
4. Add a `check_*` function to [polaris_checks/checks.py](../polaris_checks/checks.py),
   with a detection test in
   [test_checks.py](../polaris_checks/test_checks.py), for every finding a
   repository-level check can catch, so the regression is caught on every
   run.
5. Update the [threat model](design/threat-model.md) to absorb the
   findings.
