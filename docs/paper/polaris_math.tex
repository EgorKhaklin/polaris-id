\begin{abstract}
This document restates the Polaris reference implementation as a set of definitions and
properties. It is a translation, not a proof: each property carries a mark saying how the
repository supports it, and a property marked \emark{enforced} is enforced by a named object in
the schema or the code, not proved. Polaris is a reference implementation on notional data. It
is not deployed or audited, and the only certification is of one component: \code{polaris-oid4vp
1.0.0rc7} is OpenID Certified to the OpenID4VP 1.0 + HAIP 1.0 Verifier profile (24 September 2026).
Nothing below claims more.
\end{abstract}

Mark & Meaning\\
\midrule

\emark{tested} & a test or drill exercises it; nothing forbids a violation outside the tested inputs\\
\emark{measured} & a number observed on stated hardware at a stated version\\

\end{tabularx}

\section*{0.1 A model, not the system}

The equations below are a projection of Polaris, not Polaris. Write $\mathcal R$ for the system as it
exists in the world (the code, the institutions that would run it, the people it would describe),
$\mathcal O$ for what can be observed or evidenced about it, and $\mathcal M$ for this formal model.
The relationship this document assumes is
\[
\mathcal R \;\xrightarrow{\;\Omega\;}\; \mathcal O \;\xrightarrow{\;\Pi\;}\; \mathcal M,
\qquad \Pi(X)\neq X,
\qquad \mathcal M\not\equiv\mathcal R .
\]
$\Omega$ is observation: tests, drills, measurements, and what outside parties have done. $\Pi$ is
formalisation, and it loses information. What it keeps is what mathematics states well: admissible
states, transition rules, invariants, verification equations, trust relations, bounds on what flows
where. What it cannot carry is exactly what the project exists for: whether a person can be
compelled, whether an institution is legitimate, whether an authority is independent of the one it
federates with, whether a population consents. Assigning such a thing a variable does not capture
it. The marks in Section~0 exist because of this gap: a property of $\mathcal M$ is a claim about
$\mathcal R$ only as far as the evidence in $\mathcal O$ carries it.

Several distinctions the report insists on are instances of the same inequality, and the model keeps
each of them separate rather than collapsing them:
\begin{align*}
\text{claim}&\neq\text{evidence},\\
\text{internal evidence}&\neq\text{external validation},\\
\text{signature validity } A(t)&\neq\text{current authority } R(t,\tau),\\
\text{a passing test}&\neq\text{the property it names},\\
\text{privacy}&\neq\text{freedom},\\
\text{protocol independence}&\neq\text{institutional independence}.
\end{align*}
What is true of Polaris in the world can exceed what this model establishes, and what the model
establishes can exceed what has been observed. Reading an equation here as a guarantee about people
would repeat, in mathematics, the error the report's corrections tables record in code.

\section{The system}

\begin{definition}[System]
$\PP = \langle \Per, \Tok, \Ag, \Ctx, \Ev, \Alg, \mathcal K, \mathcal L, \mathcal W, \mathcal F\rangle$:
persons, tokens (credentials), issuing authorities, the seven verification contexts, verification
events, the table of cryptographic algorithms, keys, the transparency log, its witnesses, and the
federation graph.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Token]
A token is $t=(\nu_t,p_t,a_t,\alpha_t,s_t,x_t,\mathit{prev}_t)$: its value $\nu_t$ (unique:
$\nu_t=\nu_{t'}\Rightarrow t=t'$), person $p_t\in\Per$, issuing authority $a_t\in\Ag$,
algorithm $\alpha_t\in\Alg$, status $s_t$, expiry date $x_t$ (possibly $\bot$), and the token it
succeeds, if any, with $\mathit{prev}_t\neq t$ and $p_{\mathit{prev}_t}=p_t$.
\end{definition}

\section{The token state machine}

Let $S=\{\st{reserve},\st{active},\st{dormant},\st{lost},\st{expired},\st{revoked}\}$
and
\begin{align*}
\Gamma=\{&(\st{reserve},\st{active}),(\st{reserve},\st{revoked}),(\st{active},\st{dormant}),\\
&(\st{active},\st{revoked}),(\st{active},\st{lost}),(\st{active},\st{expired})\}.
\end{align*}
\begin{property}[Legal transitions]\label{p:sm}
Every change of status is a pair in $\Gamma$: if $s_t(\tau)\neq s_t(\tau')$ for consecutive writes
then $(s_t(\tau),s_t(\tau'))\in\Gamma$. Writes that do not change the status are unconstrained by
$\Gamma$. And no write enters \st{active} past the expiry: if $s_t(\tau')=\st{active}\neq s_t(\tau)$
then $x_t=\bot\vee\tau'\le x_t$, comparing calendar dates. \emark{enforced} by the token-state
triggers; the check holds the six pairs exactly; the expiry condition since \code{1.0.0-rc.37}
(before it, reporting a credential lost could promote a reserve already past its date). Pinned by
\code{check\_token\_core\_triggers\_are\_bound}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
Nothing drives $\st{active}\to\st{expired}$ when $x_t$ passes; there is no sweeper. That is why
Property~\ref{p:auth} reads the expiry date directly rather than the status.
\end{limit}

\section{The ten constraints}

\begin{property}[C1: the audit of record is append-only] For every audit table $R$ and times
$\tau_1\le\tau_2$: $R_{\tau_1}\subseteq R_{\tau_2}$, and every row present at $\tau_1$ is unchanged at
$\tau_2$. \emark{enforced} by a \texttt{BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE} trigger on each table, whose function
raises \texttt{insufficient\_privilege}, and by the grants that keep the purge carve-out out of the
application role's reach. The grants reach every partition of the four partitioned audit tables
since \code{1.0.0-rc.40} (before it the application role could empty them through their partitions),
and the carve-out requires the purge's owner as well as its setting. Where a row is written only by a
recorder (the change records, since \code{1.0.0-rc.38}; the lifecycle log, since \code{1.0.0-rc.40}),
the application role cannot write one directly, so $R_{\tau_2}\setminus R_{\tau_1}$ holds only rows a
recorder wrote. Pinned by \code{check\_aor\_append\_only\_triggers}, \code{check\_aor\_privilege\_boundary}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C2: a zero-knowledge verification stores no token identifier] For every event
$e$ with disclosure level $d(e)\in\{\st{zk},\st{selective},\st{full}\}$:
$d(e)=\st{zk}\Rightarrow \mathit{tok}(e)=\bot$ and $d(e)=\st{full}\Rightarrow
\mathit{tok}(e)\neq\bot$; \st{selective} constrains neither. \emark{enforced} by a check
constraint; \emark{modelled}. Pinned by \code{check\_c2\_zk\_token\_null}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C3: one active token per person]
$\forall p\in\Per:\ \bigl|\{t\in\Tok: p_t=p\wedge s_t=\st{active}\}\bigr|\le 1$.
\emark{enforced} by a partial unique index on the person where the status is \st{active};
\emark{tested} with real concurrent threads; \emark{modelled}. Pinned by \code{check\_one\_active\_token\_index}.
\emark{limit}: $p\in\Per$ is an enrolment record, not a human being; one human enrolled as two
records is two elements of $\Per$ and nothing here sees it. C3 is credential uniqueness per
enrolment identity, not human uniqueness, and not Sybil resistance.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C4: failed-login counting is atomic] If $k$ failed attempts against one account
complete, in any interleaving, the counter increases by exactly $k$: no increment is lost.
\emark{enforced} by a single \texttt{UPDATE \dots\ RETURNING} under the row lock. Pinned by \code{check\_c4\_atomic\_failed\_login}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C5: no inline scripts] The set of script sources a page may execute is
$\{\texttt{'self'}\}$. \emark{enforced} by the response's Content-Security-Policy header. Pinned by \code{check\_csp\_forbids\_unsafe\_inline}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C6: disclosure is decided by the server] Whatever a client submits, the stored event
satisfies C2, and every read path returns no location for a \st{zk} event:
$d(e)=\st{zk}\Rightarrow \mathit{loc}(\mathrm{read}(e))=\bot$. \emark{enforced} in the form handler,
the C2 constraint, and every read function. Pinned by \code{check\_c6\_atlas\_redacts\_zk\_location}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C7: cryptography is data] $\Alg$ is a relation (rows of a table), each token
references a row of it, and algorithm metadata is obtained by joining that relation, never from a
constant in application code. The accepted-signer allowlist is deliberately code, a security decision.
\emark{enforced}. Pinned by \code{check\_crypto\_algorithm\_is\_data}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C8: every Atlas aggregate is bounded] For every Atlas query $q$ with a caller-supplied
count $n$, the rows returned satisfy $|q|\le\min(n, N_q)$ with a fixed $N_q$, and queries without a
caller count return a fixed shape. \emark{enforced} by route-level clamps passed into the SQL. Pinned by \code{check\_c8\_atlas\_caps}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C9: concurrency is tested with real threads] C3 and C4 hold in the interleavings
produced by real concurrent threads against a live database. \emark{tested}; this is a statement
about a test suite, not a proof over all interleavings. Pinned by \code{check\_c9\_concurrency\_threading}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[C10: identity is not money] No table and no column in the schema or its migrations
has a monetary name or type. \emark{enforced} structurally by the absence, and by a check that reads
every name. Pinned by \code{check\_c10\_no\_money\_tables}.
\end{property}
\noindent(An earlier draft wrote C10 as ``no function $\Tok\to\mathbb R$''. That is false: an expiry date
is such a function. The constraint is about what the schema can \emph{hold}, a monetary quantity,
not about numbers.)

\section{Authenticity and authorization}

\begin{definition}[Signature] $h_t=\mathrm{SHA3\text{-}256}(\nu_t)$ and
$\sigma_t=\mathrm{Sign}_{\alpha_t}(k^-_{a_t},h_t)$. Authenticity is
$A(t)=\mathrm{Ver}_{\alpha_t}(k^+_{a_t},h_t,\sigma_t)\in\bool$, a function of the token and the
published key alone, so it does not change with time.
\end{definition}

\begin{property}[Accepted algorithms] $\alpha_t\in\Alg_{\mathrm{acc}}=\{\text{ML-DSA-65},\text{ML-DSA-87}\}$;
ML-DSA-44 is refused. A development placeholder signature exists and is never authentic offline.
\emark{enforced}.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Two witnesses] At issuance, two independent ML-DSA implementations must both return
1. At verification, a verifier with implementations $V_1,\dots,V_m$ available reports authentic iff
$m\ge1$ and all available ones return 1; if two disagree it reports not authentic. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
Whether Module-LWE is hard is \emark{assumed}. Polaris makes no claim of safety against quantum
adversaries, and the classical half of a credential in migration is protected by nothing here.
\end{limit}

\begin{property}[Authorization]\label{p:auth} $R(t,\tau)=\one[s_t(\tau)=\st{active}\wedge
(x_t=\bot\vee \tau\le x_t)]$, where $\tau\le x_t$ compares calendar dates and an expiry that cannot
be read counts as passed. The relying-party answer is $U(t,\tau)=A(t)\wedge R(t,\tau)$, and
$A(t)=1$ implies nothing about $R$. \emark{enforced} in both verification endpoints; \emark{tested}.
\end{property}

\section{Offline authorization: the status assertion}

A status assertion is $\zeta=(\nu,s,\tau_i,\tau_e,\sigma)$ signed by the issuer. A verifier with
clock $\tau$, maximum window $\Delta_v$ and a credential $t$ accepts it iff
\[
\mathrm{Ver}(\sigma)=1 \;\wedge\; \nu=\nu_t \;\wedge\; k^+_\zeta=k^+_{a_t}
\;\wedge\; \tau_i\le\tau<\tau_e \;\wedge\; \tau_e-\tau_i\le\Delta_v \;\wedge\; s=\st{active}.
\]
The window is half-open. \emark{enforced}; each conjunct separately \emark{tested} (2026-09-23).
On the issuer's side the assertion states the same authorization $R(t,\tau)$ the online answer
does: a credential past its expiry is asserted \st{expired} even while its stored status is
\st{active}, and an \st{active} assertion's $\tau_e$ is no later than the end of the credential's
last valid day. \emark{enforced} since \code{1.0.0-rc.8}; before it the offline and online answers
disagreed about expired credentials.
\begin{limit}
A credential revoked at $\tau_r<\tau_e$ still passes this check for $\tau\in[\tau_r,\tau_e)$. Offline
revocation latency is therefore at most $\tau_e-\tau_i\le\Delta_v$, and no offline mechanism can do
better without a fresher assertion.
\end{limit}

\section{Privacy}

\begin{property}[Issuer-unlinkable membership] In zero-knowledge mode the holder holds a secret $w$.
The leaf for context $c$ is $\ell=\mathrm{Poseidon}(w\,\Vert\,c)$; an epoch publishes the Merkle root
$\rho$ over its leaves. A proof $\pi$ shows knowledge of $(w,\mathit{path})$ with $\ell$ in the tree
of root $\rho$, bound to the public inputs $(\rho,\text{epoch},c,\text{nonce},\text{scope},\lambda)$, and
reveals neither $w$ nor the leaf's index. The nullifier is
$\lambda=\mathrm{Poseidon}(w\,\Vert\,\text{scope}\,\Vert\,\text{epoch})$. \emark{enforced} by the
circuit; recomputed by an independent second witness; \emark{assumed}: the proof system's soundness and
zero knowledge, and Poseidon behaving as a pseudorandom function.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[What a verifier learns] Let $N$ be the number of leaves in the epoch's tree for that
context (the anonymity set). A zero-knowledge presentation identifies the holder's leaf to the
verifier with probability no better than $1/N$ plus the assumptions' advantage. In particular
$N=1$ identifies the holder. Within one scope and epoch, two presentations by one holder share
$\lambda$ (one enrolment record, one proof); across scopes or epochs the nullifiers are unlinkable under the
assumption above. \emark{assumed} bound. Since \code{1.0.0-rc.46} an epoch closes only if
$N\ge N_{\min}$ (a database setting, $20$ unless set) and a proof against an epoch with
$N<N_{\min}$ is answered \emph{privacy unavailable}, never accepted; \emark{enforced} at close and
at verification. Before it nothing enforced a minimum $N$. \emark{limit}: $N_{\min}$ bounds the
set, not what epoch timing reveals.
\end{property}
\noindent(An earlier draft bounded identification by $1/|\Per|$. The anonymity set is the epoch's leaf
set, not the population.)

\begin{property}[Pairwise identifiers] A relying party that keys its records on the handle $\psi_r$
(from the holder's bound key), or receives the login subject $\mathit{sub}_r$, where
\begin{align*}
\psi_r&=\mathrm{SHA3\text{-}256}(\texttt{polaris-pairwise/1|}\,k^+_{\mathrm{holder}}\,\texttt{|}\,r),\\
\mathit{sub}_r&=\mathrm{SHA3\text{-}256}(\texttt{polaris-pairwise/1|}\,\nu\,\texttt{|}\,\mathit{client}_r),
\end{align*}
stores values that differ for $r\neq r'$. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
A full presentation still \emph{shows} each verifier the same $(\nu,\sigma,k^+)$; two verifiers that
keep that material link on it with certainty. Correlation is bounded, not eliminated: what the design
bounds is what a verifier needs to store.
\end{limit}

\section{Duress}

\begin{property}[Duress-aware presentation] A card holds two key slots, normal and duress, selected by
two PINs of equal length, with both comparisons always run and the retry counter treated the same.
The reader at the counter receives a well-formed response under either PIN and reports the same
outcome; only the authority, which recorded both slots' public keys at personalization, decides which
slot signed, and that is where an alert can be raised. A holder proof is signed without the presented
code, so a coerced presentation is byte-indistinguishable from a consenting one. \emark{enforced} in
the card profile and emulator; \emark{tested}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
The mechanism resists a coercer who does not know it exists. It does not resist one watching the
holder, and against lawful or institutional access it is net-negative, because the duress record is
append-only (C1). It is not a claim of resistance to compulsion.
\end{limit}

\section{Federation}

\begin{definition}[Trust edges] $\mathcal F=(\Ag,E)$ with $(a_i,a_j,c,[\tau_0,\tau_1))\in E$ when a
fresh manifest signed by an active anchor of $a_i$ attests $a_j$'s key for context $c$ within that
window. Write $T(a_i,a_j,c,\tau)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{property}[No transitivity] $T(a_i,a_j,c,\tau)\wedge T(a_j,a_k,c,\tau)\not\Rightarrow
T(a_i,a_k,c,\tau)$: acceptance consults only edges from authorities the relying party itself trusts.
A foreign credential is accepted iff $A(t)\wedge \exists a: T(a,a_t,c,\tau)\wedge \neg\mathrm{Rev}(t,\tau)$,
where revocation comes from the issuer's own authentic, fresh feed. No central service sits in the
path. \emark{enforced}; each conjunct \emark{tested} (2026-09-23).
\end{property}

\section{Transparency}

\begin{property}[Append-only log] Log states $L_n=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$; $L_m\prec L_n$ iff $m\le n$ and
they agree on the first $m$ entries. A signed head is $\theta_n=(n,r_n,\tau_n,\sigma_\ell)$ with
$r_n$ the RFC~6962 Merkle root. For $m<n$ a consistency proof shows $L_m\prec L_n$; an inclusion
proof shows $x_i\in L_n$. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Equivocation is evidence] Two authentic heads $\theta_n,\theta'_n$ of the same size
with $r_n\neq r'_n$ are a proof that the log operator equivocated. A head is \emph{witnessed} when at
least $K$ distinct trusted witnesses have cosigned $(n,r_n)$. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}

\section{Delegated agents}

\begin{property}[Agent grants] A grant $\kappa$ signed by the holder names an agent key, actions
$\mathcal U\neq\varnothing$, limits and a window. A request for action $u$ with amount $m$ is usable
iff the grant is authentic and fresh, the holder key is bound by an issuer to this credential and
that binding is fresh, $u\in\mathcal U$, the limits hold (an unknown limit refuses), the holder has
not revoked the grant, and the agent signed this service's nonce for this action. A grant altered
after signing is not authentic; revoking a grant does not revoke the credential. \emark{enforced};
each conjunct \emark{tested}.
\end{property}

\section{Presentations over constrained channels}

\begin{property}[QR bounds] A QR presentation has at most $9999$ frames, at most $512$\,KiB of
compressed payload and at most $2$\,MiB after decompression, and it verifies only if the digest over
the reassembled frames matches the one the frames carry. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}

\section{Algorithm migration}

\begin{property}[No credential verifies under nothing] Let $\Alg_{\mathrm{acc}}(\tau)$ be the algorithms
accepted at time $\tau$. During a migration every active credential keeps at least one signature under
an accepted algorithm:
\[
\forall\tau\ \forall t:\ s_t(\tau)=\st{active}\;\Rightarrow\;\exists\sigma\in\Sigma_t:\
\alpha(\sigma)\in\Alg_{\mathrm{acc}}(\tau)\wedge \mathrm{Ver}(\sigma)=1 .
\]
The superseded signature keeps verifying until its algorithm's deprecation date, and deprecating an
algorithm is refused while any active credential depends on it alone. \emark{enforced}; the migration
drill samples the number of credentials violating the property at every batch boundary, not only at
the ends, and measures it at zero \emark{measured}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
Agility is issuer-side only. A deprecation date reaches no signed artifact, so a verifier cannot
learn that an algorithm is deprecated: retiring one is a software release to every relying party. The
classical half of a credential in a hybrid cutover is protected by nothing here. The mixed window of
a partial migration is unmeasured. Migrating back onto an algorithm the population left cannot re-sign
a credential already holding a deprecated signature under it, since a credential holds at most one
signature per algorithm and none changes, $|\{\sigma\in\Sigma_t:\alpha(\sigma)=a\}|\le 1$. Such
credentials are counted as blocked, stay on the signature they have, and hold the window open until
they are re-issued \emark{enforced} since \code{1.0.0-rc.9}; before it the run stopped without saying so.
\end{limit}

\section{Exchanges and timestamps}

\begin{property}[A receipt records that an exchange happened, not what was exchanged] For an exchange
of request $q$ and response $r$ between authorities $a_i$ and $a_j$ in context $c$, the signed receipt
is $J=(a_i,a_j,c,\Hs(q),\Hs(r),\mathit{via},\tau)$; neither $q$ nor $r$ is in $J$. \emark{enforced} by
the receipt's signed field set.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Timestamps] A timestamp is a signature over $(\Hs(x),\text{nonce},\tau)$; it shows
that the digest existed at $\tau$. Anchored in the transparency log, a timestamp forged later must be
absent from every witnessed head of its claimed era. \emark{enforced}.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
A digest is not a secret. For a document an adversary can guess, $\Hs(x)$ confirms the guess; a
timestamp hides only what is hard to guess. An earlier draft wrote $I(x;\vartheta)\approx0$, which is
false in exactly that case.
\end{limit}

\section{Key custody}

\begin{property}[The application does not hold the signing key] The issuer's signing key $k^-$ is held
by a custody driver behind one interface: a PKCS\#11 token, where the key is non-extractable and
signing happens inside the token, a managed key service, or, for development, a software key. The
application requests signatures and never reads $k^-$ in the first two; secrets for the driver are read
from files, and the application refuses to start if they are passed in the environment.
\emark{enforced}.
\end{property}

\section{Recovery}

\begin{property}[Recovery point and recovery time] For a backup taken continuously and restored after
a loss at $\tau$, the restored database equals the database at some $\tau-\delta$, with targets
$\delta\le300$\,s (RPO) and restoration within $14\,400$\,s (RTO). Two drills at \code{v9.192}
measured $\delta$ of 36 and 42 seconds and a database restore under three seconds, on notional data.
\emark{measured} at that version; later versions are not re-measured in this document.
\end{property}

\section{Operations and scale}

\begin{property}[Re-signing throughput] Re-signing under ML-DSA-87 with one custodied key runs at
about $345$ credentials per second on one runner, 91 percent of it signing. \emark{measured}. A
population of $3.5\times10^8$ is then about $1.0\times10^6$\,s (twelve days) on one runner; on
$64$ runners, \emph{if the rate scales linearly}, about $1.6\times10^4$\,s. The second figure is an
extrapolation, not a measurement.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Every id space outlasts the horizon] For every sequence $q$ in the schema, with
capacity $c_q\in\{2^{31}-1,\,2^{63}-1\}$ and a growth rate $r_q$ at the roadmap's targets,
$c_q/r_q\ \ge\ H$ with $H=25$ years; a sequence whose table does not grow with the population is
declared bounded instead, with its reason. \emark{enforced} by \code{check\_capacity\_model} over
every sequence since \code{1.0.0-rc.14}. Before it the check sized nine of forty, and three 32-bit
sequences on the enrollment path had $c_q/r_q$ of $9.8$, $9.8$ and $14.7$ years. The rates are
assumptions stated beside each figure, except the per-verification and per-epoch ones.
\end{property}

\section{Analytics}

\begin{property}[The Atlas] Atlas outputs are aggregates over events, bounded by C8
\emark{enforced}, with the locations of \st{zk} events withheld by C6 \emark{enforced}. That no
output carries a per-person row is the design; no single check pins it.
\end{property}
\begin{limit}
Aggregates are information about people. The Atlas is bounded, not information-free, and an earlier
draft's claim that its output has zero mutual information with the population is false.
\end{limit}

\section{The assurance layer}

For each documented guarantee $g$ with a check $I_g$ and a detection test $D_g$: $D_g$ asserts
$I_g$ passes on a good fixture and fails on a broken one. That establishes \emph{fixture}
sensitivity. Mechanism sensitivity is the stronger property
\[
\forall \mu\in M_g:\quad I_g(\mu(\PP))=0,
\]
for a set $M_g$ of mutations that break $g$'s mechanism in the real tree. On 2026-09-23 held-out
rounds found many $\mu$ with $I_g(\mu(\PP))=1$, and the repairs add tests until the listed mutations
are caught. A passing test implies nothing beyond the inputs it exercises: $T_g=1\not\Rightarrow g$.
\emark{measured}; the rounds and their counts are recorded in the report.

\section{The operating contract}

A change $\Delta\PP$ to product behaviour is admitted iff it has exactly one qualifying reason:
\[
\Delta\PP\ \text{admitted}\iff E_{\mathrm{interop}}\vee E_{\mathrm{user}}\vee E_{\mathrm{security}}
\vee \exists z:\ \mathrm{Promise}(z)=1\wedge \mathrm{Impl}(z)=0,
\]
the last being an executable counterexample to a published promise. Progress is the set of external
dependencies $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{ext}}$. At this writing it holds one stock third-party wallet whose
presentation the published verifier accepted, one hosted conformance run (awaiting human review, not
passed), and one third-party test corpus; there is no audit, no pilot, no outside relying party and
no outside user. $|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{int}}|\to\infty\not\Rightarrow|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{ext}}|>0$.

\section*{What this document does not do}

It does not prove the properties; the marks say what supports each one. It omits a composite
``integrity score'': an earlier draft wrote Polaris as the maximiser of a product
$A\cdot R\cdot Z\cdot S$, but no such objective is defined, measured or optimised anywhere in the
repository, and a formula that looks like a result would be read as one.
