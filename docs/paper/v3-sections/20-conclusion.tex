\section{Conclusion: the whole town}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Every ring has now been walked, and the whole can be said in three parts: what Polaris does today, what it does not, and what it is trying to become.

\begin{description}
  \item[What Polaris can do today.] Issue, sign, verify, revoke, present, federate, log, witness,
    exchange, timestamp and sign documents, re-sign a population, wind a pilot back, be installed from
    a public registry, and accept a presentation from a wallet it had never met, all on notional
    data, all drilled in continuous integration (Appendix~\ref{app:status}).
  \item[What it cannot do today.] Hold real identity data, run on hardware tokens or a hardware
    module, prove independence of institutions or implementations, prevent correlation on a plain or
    interoperable presentation, resist a coercer who is the institution, or claim any property that
    only one wallet, one hosted suite and one test corpus have tested from outside
    (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item[What it is ultimately trying to become.] Identity infrastructure in which no important claim
    has to be taken on the word of a central application, because authority, state, history, privacy
    and the system's own behaviour are exposed to independent verification and constrained by layers
    that do not share each other's assumptions, and because the last of those layers is other
    people's software.
\end{description}

  \adjustbox{max width=0.84\textwidth,max totalheight=0.55\textheight,center}{\input{v3-figures/layer-ladder}}%
  \caption{The ladder. From the person, who exists outside the software, to the

The town's register is small. The walls around it are what make it safe to keep. And the reason the
system has so many rings is not that its builders enjoyed rings: each one was added when the ring
inside it was found to be correct and still unable to prove what a society would need to know.

\begin{center}\small\itshape\color{navymid}
A record of a person is not the person.\\[1pt]
A promise enforced only by the people who run the system is not a promise.\\[1pt]
An entry in the register is not proof of who made it.\\[1pt]
A verdict from the issuer is not a verdict anyone can check.\\[1pt]
A credential's authenticity is not its current authorization.\\[1pt]
Current authorization is not privacy.\\[9pt]
Being verified is not being followed.\\[1pt]
Federation is not institutional independence.\\[1pt]
A transparency log is not protection from a split view.\\[1pt]
A receipt for an exchange is not a copy of the letter.\\[1pt]
A correct system is not a system that survives.\\[9pt]
Watching the system is not watching the people in it.\\[1pt]
A green test is not proof that the test asked the right question.\\[1pt]
Privacy is not freedom from coercion.\\[1pt]
Proving a credential is not proving a person.\\[1pt]
An idea worth building is not a reason to build it.\\[1pt]
Putting known pieces together is not inventing them.\\[1pt]
A limit written down is not a limit removed.
\end{center}

Polaris is the accumulation of those sentences
into mechanisms, and the honest map of where the accumulation stands: at version \code{1.0.0-rc.7},
a release candidate, which is the number for a system whose every internal condition for 1.0 holds
and which no operator other than its author has yet run.

\vspace{10pt}\motif{And a system that has checked itself is not a system that anyone else has checked.}
