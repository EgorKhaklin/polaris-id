% A figure from the v3-figures directory, never wider than the text and never scaled
% smaller than the width demands. Two placements, and only two. \figfile puts the figure
% exactly where the text introduces it, after the paragraph that names it; when it does
% not fit the rest of the page it moves whole to the next, and the space it leaves is the
% price of never sitting between the lines of a paragraph. \figpage gives a tall figure a
% page of its own, the page after the paragraph that introduces it; the text fills its
% page, the figure has the next, and the discussion continues after. Version 3 first
% placed figures at the foot of text pages, which kept the page count down and cut every
% paragraph that ran on to the next page. Readability over page count: neither placement
% shares a page break with a paragraph.
\newcommand{\figfile}[3]{\setlength{\intextsep}{6pt plus 2pt minus 2pt}\begin{figure}[H]\centering
  \adjustbox{max width=\textwidth,max totalheight=0.88\textheight,center}{\input{v3-figures-ru/#1}}%
  \caption{#2}\label{#3}\end{figure}}
% \figfit: the same, with a height cap for the one figure that missed the page of its paragraph
% by fifteen points; the cap is a three per cent reduction, within reading comfort.
\newcommand{\figfit}[4]{\setlength{\intextsep}{6pt plus 2pt minus 2pt}\begin{figure}[H]\centering
  \adjustbox{max width=\textwidth,max totalheight=#1\textheight,center}{\input{v3-figures-ru/#2}}%
  \caption{#3}\label{#4}\end{figure}}
% \figfloat: a figure allowed to float to the top of the next page, for one that is too tall to
